Comparative Analysis

Topic Area

Demographic effects on quality of life.

Research Question

How does one’s socioeconomic status relate to the perceived quality of life of adults in the United States?

Datasets Considered

  • 2024 BRFSS Survey Data and Documentation
  • Smart Location Database
  • Current Employment Statistics

Trustworthiness and Reputability

Smart Location Database

In the Smart Location Database, it doesn’t explicitly say where the EPA got its data. It says the numbers were calculated from “other sources”, but never says or cites those sources. Also the years it’s been updated are a bit confusing because the page says it was last updated in 2013, but uses 2019 Census maps. When you try to dig deeper on the Walkability Index and the Smart Location Calculator, it asks you to email them on their data and methodology.

2024 BRFSS

The BRFSS has official documentation from the CDC, including its survey methods, questionnaires, sampling procedures, and data descriptions. This makes the dataset reputable because it is collected by a federal public health agency using standardized methods.

Although the data source label explains how information is collected, it does not provide specific response rates, the amount of missing data for individual variables, or how accurate participants’ self-reported answers are. Because BRFSS uses telephone surveys, some people may not be reached or may choose not to participate. Also, participants might misremember or inaccurately report their health information. These issues could affect the accuracy of the results, even though the CDC uses survey weights to reduce certain biases.

Supplemental 2023 SAHIE Estimates

The Current Employment Statistics (CES) program is a Federal-State cooperative effort in which monthly surveys are conducted to provide estimates of employment, hours, and earnings based on payroll records of business establishments. The CES survey is based on approximately 119,000 businesses and government agencies representing approximately 629,000 individual worksites throughout the United States.

CES data reflect the number of nonfarm, payroll jobs. It includes the total number of persons on establishment payrolls, employed full- or part-time, who received pay (whether they worked or not) for any part of the pay period that includes the 12th day of the month. Temporary and intermittent employees are included, as are any employees who are on paid sick leave or on paid holiday. Persons on the payroll of more than one establishment are counted in each establishment. CES data excludes proprietors, self-employed, unpaid family or volunteer workers, farm workers, and household workers. Government employment covers only civilian employees; it excludes uniformed members of the armed services.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) of the U.S. Department of Labor is responsible for the concepts, definitions, technical procedures, validation, and publication of the estimates that State workforce agencies prepare under agreement with BLS.

Degree of Relevance to the Research Question

Supplemental 2023 SAHIE Estimates

There is some marginal level of relevance to the topic at hand, as this data set offers insight into how the job market stands, and through which methods a majority of individuals are making a living.

2024 BRFSS

Since BRFSS collects self-reported survey data, researchers can identify patterns and relationships between demographics, behaviors, and health outcomes. However, they cannot establish cause and effect from these observations alone.

In a perfect world, I would be able to add the actual verified medical records or clinical measurements to compare against participants’ self-reported health information. This could help researchers understand whether survey responses reflect actual health conditions as well as drastically increasing confidence in the validity of data before making any conclusions.

Ethical Soundness

Population Coverage

With recent developments with respect to the BLS, there is some level of disingenuity to how the data is collected recently, and how truly accurate it is as a whole.

Interpretation of Results

Another concern is that the pure data results could reinforce negative stereotypes about lower-income populations that accidentally implies causation through correlation. We should take note to avoid assuming that socioeconomic status alone determines someone’s health or quality of life.

Scope

Smart Location Database

In the Smart Location Database, one row represents one census block group, as in a small neighborhood-sized area, along with its own set of calculated demographic, employment, and built environment variables.

Supplemental 2023 SAHIE Estimates

Each row represents an individual industry within a county, including the appending of the entire state itself towards the end of the dataset.

2024 BRFSS

BRFSS represents adults aged 18 and older living in noninstitutional settings across participating U.S. states and territories. The 2024 dataset includes 457,670 respondents but excludes Tennessee and people living in institutions such as prisons or nursing homes.

This dataset is relevant to our research question because it contains both socioeconomic and health-related variables, allowing us to compare health outcomes across different income, education, and employment groups.

Which Source Best Fits Our Research Question?

Selected Source: 2024 BRFSS

We decided to choose the 2024 BRFSS dataset to answer our research question because it includes information about socioeconomic factors, such as income, education, and employment, along with physical and mental health outcomes. This allows us to compare how quality of life differs among adults from different socioeconomic backgrounds.

The dataset also includes over 450,000 respondents across the United States, giving us a large sample to identify patterns and relationships. Although the information is self-reported and cannot establish cause and effect, it provides a reliable starting point for understanding how socioeconomic status relates to physical and mental health.

Comparison with SAHIE

The CES offers a marginal perspective to how the economy looks in the state, but does not provide any individual detail with respect to the quality of life of the individual.

Comparison with the Smart Location Database

When it comes to the research question, the BRFSS dataset has a wider scope of socioeconomic qualities we can choose from. With the Smart Location Database, it is more specific to one area, which is more focused on the accessibility of public transportation.
